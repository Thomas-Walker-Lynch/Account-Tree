Purpose

This document describes how sound reaches the accounts of an account tree. It is a companion to design_Account-Tree.html, whose terms it uses, and it replaces that document's Lease section. The records, the requests, the commands and the router, which carries out the connection tables, are built; the window's Sound panel is not. The section State of the code says which is which, and To verify lists what is to be tried by hand on IdealX before the code that depends on it.

The aim: programs in several accounts play at the same time, and a voice call in one account hears the microphone, while no account can listen to another except where a person chose to let it, reach another through the sound system, or touch the sound device. The person decides, account by account and program by program, what goes where, and those decisions are kept with the storage, so they survive logout and come along when the storage is plugged into another system.

Term

Three words carry the three layers of control, and in this document, in the code and in the commands, each means this and nothing else carries its meaning:

  • grant: a login account's grant of sound, which the system issues to it and revokes, kept in the system database. One grant covers the whole tree.
  • permit: an account's permit for one end of one bus, or for one device, which an account above it issues to it and revokes, kept in the storage database. A permit held is in force, or not.
  • connect: to join an endpoint to a bus end. A connection is a line of an account's connection table that does so, and takes effect only while the account holds the permits it needs in force.

Grant and permit are nouns and nothing else. In English both are verbs too, and "the account is granted" or "the stream is permitted" would read as an action of that word and blur the noun. So a grant or a permit is issued, held and revoked: the system issues a grant, a login account holds it, the system revokes it; an account issues a permit to a child account, the child holds it, the account revokes it. The verbs are shared, and the nouns say which layer is meant. The third layer has its own noun and verb already: a connection connects.

The other terms:

  • hub: the login account at the top of a tree. Its PipeWire holds the sound devices, which logind gives to its session at the seat, and carries the tree's buses. The login account is for administering its child accounts; it plays and hears nothing by default.
  • bus: a summing node in the hub, with a gather end and a broadcast end. What is connected to the gather end is added together, and everything connected to the broadcast end receives that sum. A tree has any number of buses, each numbered and named, made only from the login account's window.
  • endpoint: what is connected to a bus end: the streams of a program, named by a pattern, or a device of the hub, named with @.
  • device: the hub's output or input, as a PipeWire node. @output and @input are the output and input the login account has chosen in its ordinary sound settings, such as GNOME's, followed as the choice changes.
  • connection table: an account's list of lines, each connecting an endpoint to a bus end.
  • router: Account-Tree's program, running under each account that uses sound, which carries out that account's connection table.
  • bit bucket: where a stream goes that no line connects: a null sink that discards what it is given, and a null source that gives silence.

Two layers

Account to bus, enforced. Whether an account reaches an end of a bus is decided by its permits and enforced by the kernel. For each bus end an account holds a permit in force for, there is a FIFO between that account and the hub, which only the two can open. Sound flows through it in one direction, from the account at a gather end and to the account at a broadcast end; the kernel keeps every other account out of the FIFO, and the tunnels at its two ends keep the direction. A child account holds no descriptor to a sound device and is not a client of the hub's PipeWire, so it cannot list, record or reroute anything there.

Program to bus, convenience. Within an account, which of its own streams go to which of the bus ends it reaches is decided by its connection table, which names programs by pattern. A program chooses its own name, and a person who renames a media player firefox has it treated as Firefox; that is his choice, in his account. Each account's router connects only streams of its own account, and a line naming a bus end the account has no permit in force for has no effect.

Two consequences of the first layer guide every permit given. A permit on a broadcast end is permission to hear everything gathered onto that bus, from every branch of the tree. A permit on a gather end is permission to be heard by every account listening to that bus, which on the microphone bus means putting sound into another account's voice call.

Grant

The system issues grants to login accounts only, with Account-Tree_admin sound grant issue <login account>, and revokes one with sound grant revoke. A grant is the system's: a storage plugged into another system brings no grant with it. It brings everything else, its buses, its permits and every account's connection table, all kept in its storage database. On a system that has issued no grant to the login account the tree's sound configuration lies dormant; when the administrator issues one, the whole tree comes up for sound as it was configured. Account-Tree sound show and sound bus show print, first, whether the login account holds the grant.

Permit

There are two kinds of permit:

  • a bus end permit, for one end, gather or broadcast, of one bus: the account can connect endpoints to that end;
  • a device permit, for @output or @input: the account can connect that device to bus ends it holds bus end permits for.

The login account holds every permit by default, so that it can issue them down the tree. Every other account starts with none. A permit is issued to an account and revoked by an account above it, never by the account itself: in the window, by its parent, on the parent's window, as every window acts with the authority of its own account; with the command, by any account above it. The login account also revokes and issues its own. An account that holds a permit, in force or not, issues and revokes the same permit for the accounts below it, so putting a child on a bus end issues it a permit it too can issue down.

A permit is in force when the account holds it and its parent holds the same permit in force, or, for the login account, when it holds the grant. Which permits are held is recorded; whether one is in force is worked out from the records each time, so nothing is written when a grant is issued or a permit above is revoked.

Three-state mark

  • blank: not held;
  • gray: held, not in force, because the permit above it is not in force, or because the login account holds no grant;
  • full: held, and in force.

Clicking a blank mark issues the permit, which shows gray or full according to what is above it. Clicking a gray or full mark revokes it, to blank. Without the grant every mark in the tree toggles between blank and gray. When a permit above is revoked, the marks below it turn gray and the permits stay held, and they turn full again when it is issued again. When the grant is issued, every gray mark whose chain reaches the login account turns full.

Bus

A bus has a number and a name, and nothing else: what it gathers and where its sum goes are lines in connection tables. Only the login account's window makes, renames and removes buses. A bus the login account makes comes with the login account's permits for both its ends. A bus with nothing connected to either end is valid; a new bus starts that way. What is gathered onto a bus with nothing at its broadcast end is heard by nothing. What is gathered onto a bus is summed on that bus alone, so several buses give several separate sums at the same time, and a bus with no device on it carries sound between accounts and nowhere else, which is how a person sets up a channel between accounts on purpose.

Removing a bus is refused while any account's connection table names it; the refusal lists those accounts. The bus end permits for it are deleted with it.

Default

The first time sound is configured for a tree, which is when the first permit in it is issued, the default buses are made:

1 system sound 2 system microphone

The defaults are in the code. Once copied into a storage database, that database's own rows are the authority, and a later change to the defaults in the code does not reach them.

Connection table

Every account that holds a permit, gray or full, has a connection table, edited in its window and kept in the storage database. The table is made from the default lines the first time a permit is issued to the account, and is kept for that account from then on, permits or not; a Reset to default button puts the default lines back. Each line takes effect only while the account holds full permits for it.

For now the table is text, one line to a connection, in order:

<end> <bus> <endpoint>

The end is gather or broadcast. The bus is its number or its name, a name with a space written in double quotes. The endpoint is a device, @output or @input, or a pattern: a regular expression matched in full against the program's name, the file name of its executable, which PipeWire gives as application.process.binary. A blank line, or a line beginning with #, is ignored.

The login account's table holds device lines only, since its own programs are not to use sound. Its default:

broadcast "system sound" @output gather "system microphone" @input

Every other account's default:

# every program that plays is heard on system sound gather "system sound" .* # these programs hear the microphone broadcast "system microphone" firefox|chromium|chrome|zoom

Program line

A stream that plays is matched against the table's gather program lines, and a stream that captures against its broadcast program lines. The first line, from the top, whose pattern matches and whose bus end the account holds a full permit for, connects the stream. A stream no line connects goes to the bit bucket.

Lines name programs, not process ids, so a table holds across logouts and restarts: the person wires an account once, and every new stream of a matching program is connected the same way, in every later session and on every system the storage is plugged into.

Device line

A device line needs the device permit for its device and the bus end permit for its bus end, both full. A device connection is a fact about the hub, the pair of device and bus end: the hub makes one link for each distinct pair named by a device line in force, in any account's table, and keeps it while at least one such line names it. The same line in two tables is one connection. One device on several bus ends is several links, since PipeWire gives one source to as many links as it has.

A device permit is a strong permit. An account holding @input and a gather permit on some bus sends the room's sound to every account listening to that bus.

How sound moves

In the hub

Each bus is a null node in the hub's PipeWire, support.null-audio-sink, which adds together whatever is linked into its input and gives the sum on its monitor. Its media class is Audio/Bus, a class of Account-Tree's own: the PulseAudio server of PipeWire, through which GNOME's sound settings see devices, lists only sinks and sources, so no bus is offered as an output or an input, and none can be chosen as the hub's own output and broadcast into itself. WirePlumber does not link a node of a class it does not know either, so the router makes every link to and from a bus itself, with pw-link, and removes a link between the nodes it links, the buses and the sides of the tunnels and loopbacks it joins to them, that it did not plan. It leaves every other link alone: the device side of a loopback and the bit bucket are WirePlumber's to link, and the person can choose the bit bucket as the hub's output. Should something keep making a link the router removes, the router removes it three times, then leaves it and says so in its log, so that the router and WirePlumber cannot work against each other without end. The hub's wiring therefore does not depend on WirePlumber's version or configuration.

A device line in force is a loopback, two streams joined: @output on a broadcast end is a capture stream the router links from the bus's monitor, joined to a playback stream WirePlumber gives the hub's chosen output; @input on a gather end is a capture stream WirePlumber gives the hub's chosen input, joined to a playback stream the router links into the bus. The device side is left to WirePlumber so that it follows the login account's choice as it changes, and the loopback converts between the device's channels and the bus's two.

Between the hub and an account

For each bus end an account other than the login account holds a full permit for, the request interpreter makes a FIFO, /run/Account-Tree/bus/<account>/<bus number>.<end>. PipeWire's pipe-tunnel opens a FIFO for reading and writing, whichever way it carries sound, so the FIFO gives both the account and the login account reading and writing: it is owned by the account, mode 0600, with the group root, and the login account's access is an ACL entry. A share makes another account a member of the login account's own group, and such an account cannot open it. The directories down to it are root's, mode 0711, so an account passes through to its FIFO by name and lists nothing. A FIFO whose owner or ACL entry no longer matches the accounts' system uids, as after a storage is plugged in again, is made again. Each end of a FIFO is a pipe-tunnel, with the format fixed at both ends, 16-bit stereo at 48 kHz, so the reader interprets the bytes by its own configuration and the writer has nothing to negotiate:

  • gather: in the account, a sink named for the bus, whose samples are written to the FIFO; in the hub, a stream that reads the FIFO, which the router links into the bus;
  • broadcast: in the hub, a stream the router links from the bus's monitor, which writes the FIFO; in the account, a source named for the bus, whose samples are read from the FIFO.

Each such stream in the hub carries the account's name and the bus's, so the hub's mixer shows every account on every bus as its own stream, with its own level and mute. Disconnecting one leaves the others alone, and nothing on one account's FIFO reaches another account. The hub reads a gather FIFO as fast as the account writes it: during playback the FIFO was measured holding nothing, so it adds no delay of its own; what delay there is lies in the tunnels' buffers.

Router

The router, Account-Tree_router, with its code in Sound·Router.py, runs under each account from the user unit Account-Tree_router.service, which Account-Tree_admin system configure installs in /etc/systemd/user and enables for every account but the system's own, ConditionUser=!@system. At start, and each time it is told to reload, it gives a request to the request gate, sound_route_show, and is answered with its account's program lines in force and, for each bus end the account holds a full permit for, the FIFO to use; the hub's router is answered with the tree's buses, the hub's side of every FIFO of the tree, and the device connections in force. An account in no tree, or in a tree whose login account holds no grant, is answered no route, and its router waits to be told to reload. Communication across the privilege boundary goes through the gate; the FIFOs are files because they are the sound channels themselves, and their permissions are the enforcement.

From its answer the router makes a plan, which Account-Tree_router plan prints:

  • nodes: the buses, in the hub, and the bit bucket, made by one pw-cli the router keeps running, so they go when the router does;
  • processes: each tunnel and each loopback, a pipewire -c <conf> process of its own, its configuration in the account's runtime directory, so that one is started or stopped, and a dead one started again, without restarting the account's PipeWire;
  • links, in the hub: between the buses and the tunnels and loopbacks;
  • rules, in another account: its program lines, each naming the bus end's sink or source.

The router watches its account's PipeWire with pw-dump --monitor. As nodes and ports come and go it makes the links that are missing; and to each new stream of a program it gives a target with pw-metadata, as a patch tool does when a person moves a stream by hand: the bus end the first matching line names, matched against the program's application.process.binary, or the bit bucket. A stream is given its target once, when it appears, so a person who moves it afterwards is not overruled, until the router reloads and gives every stream its target again. The streams and nodes Account-Tree makes carry the property account_tree.stream, and the router leaves them alone.

The request interpreter, after any change, tells each affected router to reload, with systemctl --user --machine=<account>@ reload Account-Tree_router.service, which sends it SIGHUP. The signal carries no data; the router asks again.

Bit bucket

In a child account the bit bucket's two null nodes are the default output and input, by their session priority, and with the bus sinks and sources they are the only audio devices the account has, since it has no access to /dev/snd. Its sound settings show the bus ends it reaches by the buses' names, and a person in that account can move a stream among them with the ordinary tools. A stream that appears before the router has seen it is never heard and hears nothing.

The hub has a bit bucket too, at the lowest priority, so that its default output and input stay the devices the person chose, since that choice is what @output and @input mean. Its router sends every stream of the login account's own programs to the bit bucket, and such a stream reaches the chosen device for the moment before the router moves it.

Session

The hub's PipeWire runs while the login account has its session at the seat, which is how an account tree is used: the person logs in to the login account and enters the child accounts from it. A child account's router starts with its systemd --user instance, at enter. A systemd user instance already running when the unit is first installed reads it after systemctl --user daemon-reload, and its router is then started with systemctl --user start Account-Tree_router. Two trees with sessions on one seat each have a hub, and only the hub of the seat's active session reaches the devices.

Record

In the system database:

  • resource_grant: the resource, for now always sound; the system account's name of the login account; when the grant was issued. The table was first named resource_allowance; that table stays defined in the schema, empty and unread, because records written before the rename write into it and the record's replay runs them.

In the storage database:

  • sound_bus: the login account's database uid, the bus's number and name, and when it was made.
  • sound_permit_bus_end: one row for each bus end permit an account holds: the account's database uid, the bus number, the end, and when it was issued, in the column made.
  • sound_permit_device: one row for each device permit an account holds: the account's database uid, the device, and when it was issued, in the column made.
  • sound_connection: the lines of each account's connection table: the account's database uid, the line number, the end, the bus number, the kind of endpoint, program, device or comment, and the pattern, the device or the comment's text.

A revoked permit has no row. Whether a permit is in force is worked out from these rows and the grant. Deallocation deletes an account's rows. The tables are written for sound alone; the pattern of a grant to the login account, permits down the tree and connections by table fits other resources too, and once a second resource shows what they have in common, the tables and the code are to be made general.

Request and command

  • sound_show(account_name, >sound): the tree's buses with the accounts holding each end; the marks of the permits of the account and of its child accounts; the account's connection table. The window reads this.
  • sound_permit_issue(account_name, permit) and sound_permit_revoke(account_name, permit), where the permit is a bus end, as 1.gather, or a device: issue it to the account, or revoke it. Accepted when the asking account is the login account, or holds that permit, in force or not, and the account named is below it.
  • sound_connection_set(account_name, text) and sound_connection_reset(account_name): replace an account's table, or put the default lines back. Accepted for the account itself or an account above it, while the account has a table. A program line in the login account's table is refused.
  • sound_bus_set(bus, name) and sound_bus_remove(bus): make or rename a bus, or remove it. Accepted for the login account alone.
  • sound_route_show(>route): what the asking account's router needs, as described under Router.

The commands put the verb before the account, so that a child account named bus cannot be taken for the word:

Account-Tree sound show <account> Account-Tree sound permit issue|revoke <account> <bus> gather|broadcast Account-Tree sound permit issue|revoke <account> @output|@input Account-Tree sound connection show <account> Account-Tree sound connection set <account> <file> Account-Tree sound connection reset <account> Account-Tree sound bus show Account-Tree sound bus set <bus> <name> Account-Tree sound bus remove <bus> Account-Tree_admin sound grant issue|revoke <login account> Account-Tree_admin sound grant show

Window

The window's Sound panel has three parts:

  • Bus: the tree's buses, numbered, with their names, and for each end the accounts holding a permit for it; then the devices, with the accounts holding a permit for each. In the login account's window, each name is an entry, renaming the bus on Enter, each bus has a button removing it, and a last row makes a bus from a number and a name.
  • Permit: a row for the window account and one for each of its child accounts; a column for each end of each bus and one for each device, holding the three-state marks as check boxes: empty, blank; a dash, gray; checked, full. Clicking a blank mark issues the permit, clicking another revokes it. A mark the window account cannot change is greyed out: in the login account's window every mark can change, its own row's included; in another account's window, its own row never changes, and its child accounts' marks change for the permits it holds, gray or full, as its own row shows.
  • Connection: the window account's connection table, as text, with Save and Reset to default. A table that is refused stays as typed, and the Request panel says which line is wrong. An account with no table yet is told it is made with its first permit.

Above the three parts the panel says whether the login account holds the grant, and, when it holds none, the command that issues one. Each change is a request, and the panel is read again after it, with sound_show; a Read again button reads it on demand. Opening a child account's window from this one gives the same panel one generation lower, running under the child account and acting with its authority.

Life cycle

  • Grant issued: the hub's router is told to reload, and makes the buses and the device connections; every router in the tree with a full permit then connects.
  • Grant revoked: every router in the tree is told to reload, and disconnects; the records stay.
  • Permit issued or revoked, table saved: the FIFOs are made or deleted, and the routers of the account, of the accounts below it whose marks change, and of the hub, are told to reload.
  • Enter: the account's systemd --user instance starts its router.
  • Unplug: the tree's FIFOs are deleted before the system accounts are, so no FIFO outlives the uid it was made for; the routers end with the other processes.
  • Deallocate: the account's FIFOs and rows are deleted, and the hub's router is told to reload.

State of the code

  • Built: the records, in the system database and the storage database; Sound.py, the permits and their marks, the connection tables, the buses, and making and deleting the FIFOs; the requests, with their checks in the request guard; the Account-Tree sound and Account-Tree_admin sound commands; the FIFOs made again at attach, and deleted at unplug and deallocation.
  • Built: the router, Account-Tree_router and Sound·Router.py, and its user unit. Tested on a machine running PipeWire 1.0.5 and WirePlumber 0.4 under two accounts, each with its own session bus and no systemd, with null nodes standing in for the speaker and the microphone: a child's program played through system sound to the stand-in speaker, two children played there at once, the stand-in microphone was heard by a child's program through system microphone, a program no line matched went to the bit bucket, and revoking a permit, revoking the grant and issuing it again took effect at the routers' reload. Run on IdealX: the hub's router, and a child account playing through system sound to the speakers.
  • Built: the window's Sound panel. Tried on the test machine under X: issuing and revoking permits from the login account's window and from a child account's, the gray mark with the grant revoked, making a bus, and a refused connection table.

To verify

Tried on the test machine, PipeWire 1.0.5 and WirePlumber 0.4, as State of the code describes:

  1. The pipe-tunnel: a FIFO written by a child account's sink and read in the login account carries the sound intact, in both directions, and two children playing at once are both heard on the speaker, each at full level.
  2. The FIFO adds no delay: it held nothing during playback. Whether a video's sound keeps to its picture is still to be seen on IdealX.
  3. pipewire -c <conf>, run as a process of its own, makes its tunnel in the account's running PipeWire, and ending it removes the tunnel.
  4. A node of media class Audio/Bus is not listed by PipeWire's PulseAudio server, which GNOME's sound settings read, so the buses are kept out of the outputs and inputs offered.
  5. Setting a stream's target with pw-metadata moves a stream already playing.
  6. A bus with streams gathered onto it and nothing at its broadcast end runs, clocked by PipeWire's dummy driver: the streams play at their own pace.
  7. A child account's WirePlumber, with no access to sound devices, starts and links its streams.

Tried on IdealX: the user unit starting the router; a child account's program heard on the speakers. Still to try there, PipeWire 1.2 or later and WirePlumber 0.5: the routers reloading when a permit or a table changes, and at enter; the loopbacks following a change of output in GNOME's sound settings, now that the router leaves WirePlumber's links alone; pw-metadata targets under WirePlumber 0.5; the program name Firefox and Chrome give as application.process.binary, which the default line for the microphone matches in full; and a video's lip sync.

Later feature

  • @node <node name>, naming one particular device, so that two outputs or two inputs serve different buses at once; with it, an error on a bus whose arrangement the hardware does not support.
  • A warning before saving a table that makes a loop: a program listening to a bus and playing onto a bus whose sum reaches back to the first.
  • Mix-minus: giving an account's broadcast connection the sum of its bus without that account's own contribution.
  • Patterns matched against other stream properties, such as application.name or media.role.
  • A graphical editor for the connection table, in place of the text.
